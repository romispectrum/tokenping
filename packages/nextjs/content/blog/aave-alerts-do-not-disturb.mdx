## Why can an alert reach your phone and still not wake you?

An alert passes four steps before you hear it: Tokenping sends it, the service (Signal, Telegram or the mail provider) delivers it, the app on your phone shows it, and your phone's notification rules decide whether it makes a sound. The last two steps are where a delivered alert most often goes unnoticed, because night modes and battery savers are designed to keep you from being disturbed.

Tokenping checks your health factor every 5 minutes and, while it is below your threshold, sends a message on each channel you chose at every check, as [the setup guide describes](/blog/aave-health-factor-alert-setup#how-often-will-you-be-alerted). Every one of those messages meets the same filters: if your night mode silences the app, it silences all of them, and you find them in the morning.

| What holds the alert back                       | Where             | What happens to the alert                        | Where to change it                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Focus, Do Not Disturb, Sleep or Bedtime mode    | iPhone, Android   | Shown without sound, or only once you unlock     | The mode's allowed apps and people                           |
| Scheduled Summary                               | iPhone            | Waits for the next summary                       | The app's notification delivery setting                      |
| Silent mode                                     | iPhone            | No sound                                         | The ringer switch or volume                                  |
| Battery saver, Adaptive Battery, sleeping apps  | Android           | Late, or only when you open the app              | The app's battery and background settings                    |
| Notification cooldown                           | Android           | Sounds softened when many notifications arrive   | Settings > Notifications > Notification cooldown             |
| Muted chat or notification profile              | Signal, Telegram  | No sound                                         | The chat's settings                                          |
| A linked desktop app that showed it first       | Signal, Telegram  | The phone may stay quiet                         | Quit the desktop app at night                                |
| Spam folder, a quiet inbox tab, priority filter | Email             | Delivered, but no notification                   | Mail filters and the mail app's notification settings        |

Menu names differ between phone makers and system versions. The steps below follow Apple's, Google's, Samsung's, Signal's and Telegram's own help pages as of 25 Oct 2026.

## How do you let an alert through Focus or Do Not Disturb on an iPhone?

Add the app that carries your alert to the allowed apps of every Focus that can be on while you sleep, usually Do Not Disturb and Sleep:

1. Open **Settings > Focus** and choose **Do Not Disturb**.
2. Under **Allow Notifications From**, tap **Apps** and add **Signal** or **Telegram**, or the mail app your alerts arrive in.
3. Do the same for **Sleep** and any other Focus you use at night. Sleep turns on by itself with the sleep schedule set in the Health app, so a phone that rings at 11 p.m. can filter the same alert at 3 a.m.
4. In **Settings > Notifications**, open that app and set **Notification Delivery** to **Immediate Delivery**. With **Scheduled Summary**, its notifications wait for the next summary.

Three settings look like shortcuts but don't help here:

- **Time Sensitive Notifications.** A Focus has a switch that lets apps break through with notifications they mark as time sensitive, and an app offers it only if it supports it. Neither Signal nor Telegram says in its documentation that it marks messages as time sensitive, so add the app itself rather than relying on this switch.
- **Emergency Bypass.** Apple describes it as part of a contact's ringtone and text tone, for that person's calls and messages. It doesn't cover a Signal or Telegram chat with Tokenping.
- **Silent mode.** With the ringer on silent or the volume all the way down, notification sounds don't play even when a Focus lets the app through. Keep the ringer on and let the Focus silence everything else.

Signal's iPhone page adds two settings that can delay messages: **Background App Refresh** should stay on for Signal, and **Low Power Mode** can interfere with receiving messages on time.

## How do you let an alert through Do Not Disturb on Android?

On a Google Pixel, open **Settings > Modes > Do Not Disturb**, find **Notification filters**, and under **Apps** add the app your alerts arrive in. **Bedtime** is a separate mode with its own schedule, so check it the same way. On a Samsung phone, the Do Not Disturb settings have an **Allowed during Do not disturb** section where you can pick apps and contacts.

Android also lets important chats through as **priority conversations**: Google's Pixel help says they still show when Do Not Disturb is on, and you manage them under **Settings > Notifications > Conversations**. Only apps that send chats as conversations appear there. If your Signal or Telegram chat with Tokenping is listed, setting it to priority is the narrowest exception you can make.

Three more Android settings can make an alert late or quiet:

- **Battery savers.** Google says Adaptive Battery may delay notifications, Battery Saver may delay low-priority ones, and Extreme Battery Saver pauses most apps so they send none. Signal's troubleshooting asks Android users to exclude Signal from battery optimization, allow it unrestricted background data and, on Android 9 and later, turn off Adaptive Battery; Telegram's FAQ asks you to add Telegram to the battery saver's exceptions. Google recommends leaving most apps optimized, so exempt only the one app your alerts use.
- **Samsung's sleeping apps.** Apps on the **Deep sleeping apps** list never run in the background and only work when you open them. Move Signal or Telegram to **Never sleeping apps** under the battery's **Background usage limits**.
- **Notification cooldown.** Newer Android versions soften sounds and pop-ups when many notifications arrive in a short time. Priority conversations are exempt. To hear every alert at full volume, turn it off under **Settings > Notifications > Notification cooldown**.

## Which Signal settings can silence an alert?

The first Tokenping alert on Signal arrives as a message request, because Tokenping isn't in your contacts. Accept it; if you block the sender, no alert reaches you (the [setup guide's channel step](/blog/aave-health-factor-alert-setup#step-4-create-an-alert-and-choose-your-channels) covers this). After that, three Signal settings can keep the chat quiet:

- **A muted chat.** Signal mutes a chat for 1 hour, 8 hours, 1 day, 7 days or always, and shows a mute icon next to it. Check that the Tokenping chat has none.
- **Notification profiles.** On Android and Signal Desktop, a notification profile lets only chosen people and groups through while it is on, and it can switch on by schedule; a purple icon at the top of the chat list shows it is active. If you use one at night, add the Tokenping chat to it. Signal says notification profiles are not available on iPhone.
- **A linked Signal Desktop.** Signal's troubleshooting asks you to check whether a linked desktop has already viewed the message. If Signal is open on a computer at night, the phone may not alert you; quit Signal Desktop before you sleep.

## Which Telegram settings can silence an alert?

Tokenping sends its Telegram alerts as ordinary messages with sound, so the settings that matter are yours:

- **Mute and silent notifications.** Telegram lets you mute a chat, mute it for a set time, or keep notifications but turn off their sound. Open the chat with the Tokenping bot and check both.
- **Exceptions.** Under **Notifications and Sounds**, Telegram lists the chats whose settings differ from your defaults. If the bot's chat is on that list, check what it is set to.
- **Android basics.** Telegram's FAQ asks you to keep notifications on in **Notifications and Sounds**, set their importance to high on Android, and add Telegram to the battery saver's exceptions.
- **Other devices.** Telegram's support notes that a message already viewed on another device, or an account logged out on the phone, means no notification on the phone.

Sending /stop to the bot, or blocking it, unlinks the chat, and alerts stop on Telegram until you link it again.

## How do you keep alert emails out of the spam folder and quiet tabs?

Email is the channel most likely to be delivered without waking you, because mail apps decide which messages notify at all. An alert's subject reads "Aave Health Factor Alert - Arbitrum" (with your market's network), and a test's reads "Tokenping test message - Arbitrum"; the sender is the address you see on the test.

- **Gmail.** If an alert lands in Spam, mark it **Not spam** or add the sender to your contacts, so the next one stays in the inbox. A filter on the sender's address with **Never send it to Spam** checked does the same, and filters can be created only on a computer. Gmail's app notifies only for the **Primary** tab by default, and Gmail describes **Updates** as automated messages that may not need immediate attention, so use the same filter to keep alerts in Primary. If the app's email notifications are set to **High priority only**, an alert may never notify.
- **Apple Mail.** Add the sender to **VIP** from its contact card and turn on **VIP Alerts**, which gives that sender's mail its own notification settings.
- **Outlook for iPhone and Android.** By default it notifies only for mail in the **Focused** inbox. Move an alert to Focused and choose to always move that sender there, or set notifications to all email.

Even when it notifies, email is easy to sleep through. Pair it with Signal or Telegram rather than relying on it alone.

## How do you test your setup before you need it?

Test the way an alert will actually reach you, not just whether it arrives:

1. Turn on the Focus or mode you sleep with, lock the phone and put it where it stays at night.
2. In the dashboard, press **Send test** for each channel (see [step 7 of the setup guide](/blog/aave-health-factor-alert-setup#step-7-send-a-test-message)). You can send one test per channel every 15 minutes.
3. Check that you heard or felt it, not only that it shows in the app later.
4. To see a real alert arrive, the setup guide explains how to set the threshold just above your current health factor for one check and back again.

Repeat the test after you change phones, set up a new Focus or mode, reinstall the app or move to a new mail app. The settings that held an alert back are per device and per app, so a new phone starts from its defaults.

## Can an alert still come too late?

Yes, and phone settings are only one reason. Tokenping checks every 5 minutes, not continuously, so a sharp fall can cross from your threshold to below 1.00 between two checks. An alert also gives you time only if the threshold leaves room to act half awake, which [how much buffer a health factor gives you](/blog/safe-aave-health-factor) helps you choose, and only if you can act from where you are: [how to raise your Aave health factor](/blog/avoid-aave-liquidation) covers the options.

Tokenping checks your Aave V3 and V4 health factor every 5 minutes on 22 networks and messages you on Signal, Telegram or email while it is below the threshold you set (1.1 to 10), repeating on every check until it recovers. It is in beta and has no uptime guarantee, which is one more reason to use two channels that pass your night settings in different ways. The steps are in [how to set up a health factor alert](/blog/aave-health-factor-alert-setup), and you can [check your Aave V3 health factor](/aave) first; looking at your positions needs no payment.
