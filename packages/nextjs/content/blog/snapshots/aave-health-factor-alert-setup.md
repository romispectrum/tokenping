## 4. Worked arithmetic (recomputed by the writer with Python `decimal`)

Position: 10 WETH collateral, 12,000 USDC debt, USDC = $1.00, ETH = $2,400 (illustrative), LT = 0.83.

- HF = 10 × 2,400 × 0.83 ÷ 12,000 = 19,920 ÷ 12,000 = **1.66**.
- ETH price at health factor h: P(h) = h × 12,000 ÷ (10 × 0.83) = h × 1,445.7831…

| h    | P(h)      | Fall from $2,400 = 1 − P(h) ÷ 2,400 |
| ---- | --------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1.66 | $2,400.00 | 0.00%                               |
| 1.5  | $2,168.67 | 9.64% → 9.6%                        |
| 1.3  | $1,879.52 | 21.69% → 21.7%                      |
| 1.0  | $1,445.78 | 39.76% → 39.8%                      |

- When the first alert fires (corrected 2026-10-20): the cron compares the exact health factor with the threshold
  (`reading.healthFactorValue < threshold`, `services/notifications/runNotificationCheck.ts:148-149`, since commit
  `6305594`, 2026-10-15 01:40 UTC, about 85 minutes after the review round) and the message shows it rounded down
  (`formatHealthFactorDown`, `utils/healthFactor.ts:16-24`). So with threshold 1.5 the first alert comes at the first
  check after the HF drops below 1.5, i.e. ETH below P(1.5) = **$2,168.67** (the table row), the article's "once ETH is
  below $2,168.67". The guide said "once the health factor reads 1.49 or lower (it is rounded to two decimals) ... ETH
  below about $2,161" until 2026-10-19; that described the rounding comparison the code dropped in `6305594`.
- Further fall from the first-alert price to liquidation = 1 − P(1) ÷ P(h) = 1 − 1/h: h = 1.5 → 33.33% ("roughly
  another 33%"), h = 1.3 → 23.08% ("about 23%"). The old rounding boundaries (1.495, 1.295) gave 33.11% and 22.78%,
  the same wording.
- The table rows give the ETH price at exactly that health factor and are unaffected by the rounding rule.
- At the live oracle price ($2,415.56) the same position would read HF 1.6708 (not used in the article).
- All figures ignore interest accrual and assume USDC = $1.00, as the article says.
