## 4. Worked arithmetic (recomputed by the writer with Python `decimal`)

Position: 10 WETH collateral, 12,000 USDC debt, USDC = $1.00, ETH = $2,400 (illustrative), LT = 0.83.

- HF = 10 × 2,400 × 0.83 ÷ 12,000 = 19,920 ÷ 12,000 = **1.66**.
- ETH price at health factor h: P(h) = h × 12,000 ÷ (10 × 0.83) = h × 1,445.7831…

| h    | P(h)      | Fall from $2,400 = 1 − P(h) ÷ 2,400 |
| ---- | --------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1.66 | $2,400.00 | 0.00%                               |
| 1.5  | $2,168.67 | 9.64% → 9.6%                        |
| 1.3  | $1,879.52 | 21.69% → 21.7%                      |
| 1.0  | $1,445.78 | 39.76% → 39.8%                      |

- When the first alert fires: the cron rounds the health factor to two decimals (`toFixed(2)`) and alerts only when the
  rounded value is strictly below the threshold (§5). With threshold 1.5, a raw HF from 1.495 up to 1.5 displays as
  1.50 and sends nothing (Node: `1.4951.toFixed(2)` = "1.50", `1.4949.toFixed(2)` = "1.49"), so the first alert needs a
  raw HF below about 1.495, i.e. a displayed 1.49 or lower. P(1.495) = **$2,161.45** (fall from $2,400 = 9.94%), the
  article's "ETH below about $2,161". For 1.3 the boundary is 1.295 → $1,872.29.
- Further fall from the first-alert price to liquidation = 1 − P(1) ÷ P(h) = 1 − 1/h: h = 1.495 → 33.11% ("roughly
  another 33%"), h = 1.295 → 22.78% ("about 23%"). At the exact thresholds the figures are 33.33% and 23.08%; both
  roundings give the same wording.
- The table rows give the ETH price at exactly that health factor and are unaffected by the rounding rule.
- At the live oracle price ($2,415.56) the same position would read HF 1.6708 (not used in the article).
- All figures ignore interest accrual and assume USDC = $1.00, as the article says.
