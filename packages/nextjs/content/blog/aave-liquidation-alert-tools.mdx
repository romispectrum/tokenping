## Which Aave alert tool is right for you?

Aave liquidation monitors and position trackers differ in where they send the warning and in whether software acts on
your position for you. Who each tool is best for, based on the vendors' own pages on 19 Oct 2026:

- **Otomato:** you want alerts on Telegram or in a mobile app without a fee, and you'd rather paste a wallet address
  than connect a wallet. It covers Aave V3 on Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Polygon, Avalanche and Monad, and up to three
  wallets cost nothing.
- **HF Guard:** you want a Telegram bot with a web dashboard that reads Aave V3 on 23 networks and Aave V4 on Ethereum,
  Avalanche and Arc from a pasted address, and you accept a beta whose alerts "may be delayed", in its own words.
- **DeFi Saver Automation:** you want debt repaid automatically, and your position has at least $30,000 of debt on
  Ethereum, or $500 on Arbitrum, Optimism or Base. Most of its automations also need one collateral and one debt asset.
- **DeFi Saver Notify:** you already manage your position in DeFi Saver and want ratio alerts in its app, by email, in
  Blockscan chat or on Telegram.
- **DeFi Monitor:** you want alerts in Telegram or Discord for Aave V3 on Ethereum or Arbitrum, next to alerts for other
  lending protocols.
- **Ava:** you borrow on Ethereum or Base and want a Telegram-first assistant that prepares the repay or top-up
  transaction, with optional automatic execution.
- **A browser extension** (Aave Monitor or Aave DeFi Watcher): a toolbar badge or a desktop notification while you're at
  your computer is enough.
- **Tokenping:** you want the alert on Signal, which no other tool checked here lists, or you want Signal, Telegram and
  email side by side with a Send test button for each. It also reads every Aave V4 Spoke, including the one on Base
  that HF Guard's page doesn't list.

All of these tools watch the health factor, Aave's measure of how safe a loan is: collateral weighted by each asset's
liquidation threshold, divided by debt. Below 1.00 the position can be liquidated ([what the Aave health factor
is](/blog/aave-health-factor) covers the formula). No tool removes that risk: an alert gives you time to act, and a fast
price drop can still land between two checks.

### How often does each tool check your health factor?

Stated check intervals for Aave health factor tools range from every 5 minutes to every 6 hours, and most alert services
don't publish one. The interval is the longest a drop below your threshold can go unseen by that tool, before any
delivery delay. Intervals as stated by each vendor on 19 Oct 2026:

| Tool | Check interval, as stated by the vendor | Checks per day |
|---|---|---|
| Tokenping | Every 5 minutes | 288 |
| KeeperHub's example workflow (Aave V4) | Every 5 minutes | 288 |
| Aave DeFi Watcher | Every 5 minutes by default, changeable in settings | 288 by default |
| Ava, Auto mode | Hourly | 24 |
| Aave Monitor's iOS widgets (developer's guide) | Every 60 minutes by default, more often as risk rises | 24 or more |
| OpenClaw "aave-liquidation-monitor" skill | Every 6 hours by default, configurable | 4 |
| DeFi Monitor | No number given ("checks run continuously"); its changelog of 20 Feb 2026 adds checks triggered by Aave V3 events | Not stated |
| HF Guard | "Checked periodically", no number | Not stated |
| Otomato, DeFi Saver Notify | Not stated | Not stated |

Checks per day are 1,440 minutes divided by the interval: 1,440 ÷ 5 = 288, 1,440 ÷ 60 = 24 and 1,440 ÷ 360 = 4.
Tokenping works through saved alerts one after another in each run, so a message can arrive a little after the 5-minute
mark.

## Alerts or automation: which do you need?

Automation is the stronger choice when you can't count on being reachable at the moment it matters and your position
meets the tool's requirements. Alerts suit positions below automation minimums, networks or markets that automation
doesn't cover, and borrowers who don't want software that can move funds in their position.

An alert tool tells you that your Aave health factor is falling and leaves the fix to you. An automation tool repays
debt for you once you give it authority to act; DeFi Saver and Instadapp also set minimum sizes and charge per action,
while Ava's Auto mode charges nothing today and acts from a separate Ava smart wallet you fund. Neither prevents
liquidation in every case: alerts depend on you acting in time, automation on its trigger, check interval and
transaction going through.

**What alerts cost you** is attention, and the risk of acting late. In May 2021 a Polygon forum user tried to repay at a
health factor of 1.01, hit repeated errors, and was liquidated before the repayment went through. In December 2024 an
Aave forum user described more than an hour of stuck repay attempts followed by liquidation. An alert threshold well
above 1.0 leaves room for that kind of delay.

**A worked example on the Aave V3 Ethereum Core market** uses WETH collateral and USDC debt. The parameters were read
on-chain on 14 Oct 2026 (Ethereum block 26,194,044) and read again on 19 Oct 2026 (block 26,225,316) with the same
values: WETH liquidation threshold 83%, liquidation bonus 5%. The ETH prices are illustrative, and USDC is taken as
$1.00.

- You supply 10 WETH and borrow 12,000 USDC. If ETH falls to $1,420, the collateral is worth $14,200 and the health
  factor is 14,200 × 0.83 ÷ 12,000 = 0.982.
- A liquidator may then repay up to half the debt, 6,000 USDC, because the health factor is above 0.95 and both reserves
  involved are worth at least $2,000 (Aave V3's rule; Aave V4 liquidates toward a target health factor instead). In
  return they take 6,000 × 1.05 = $6,300 of your WETH. The liquidation costs you $300.
- DeFi Saver Automation couldn't be switched on for this position on Ethereum: 12,000 USDC of debt is below its
  $30,000 minimum there. The same position on Arbitrum, Optimism or Base clears the $500 minimum.
- Where DeFi Saver Automation can run, it charges per action. On Aave V3 its Liquidation Protection repays only enough
  debt to lift the Safety Ratio from 105% to 110%, and each $1,000 of WETH it sells for USDC costs $3 in fees (a 0.25%
  swap fee plus a 0.05% automation fee, as published on 19 Oct 2026). Gas comes on top, and so does Aave's 0.09%
  flash-loan fee when no zero-fee flash-loan source has enough liquidity.

Once you're warned, [how to raise your Aave health factor](/blog/avoid-aave-liquidation) covers the options.

## Which Aave health factor alerts cost nothing?

Aave health factor alerts that cost nothing come from Otomato, HF Guard during its beta, DeFi Monitor's no-cost tier, Ava
(nothing is billed today) and the Aave Monitor and Aave DeFi Watcher browser extensions. Their terms as of 19 Oct 2026:

- **Otomato:** core Aave V3 monitoring (health factor, liquidation risk, rates) costs nothing for up to three wallets, on
  Telegram or its phone app. Premium is planned for more wallets and for overnight phone-call escalation.
- **HF Guard:** its FAQ says it costs nothing during the beta and gives no later price. Alerts go to its Telegram bot.
- **DeFi Monitor:** a no-cost tier "for early users"; its pricing page lists only the paid plans, Pro at $4.99 a month
  ($49.99 a year) and Ultra at $9.99 a month ($99.99 a year). Alerts go to Telegram or Discord.
- **Ava:** nothing is billed today. Its pricing page says Ava is exploring a usage-based fee only on actions that create
  value, such as protection that stops a liquidation, and not on monitoring or warnings.
- **Aave Monitor and Aave DeFi Watcher:** browser extensions that cost nothing and warn you only while the browser is
  running.
- **DeFi Notifications app** (Hexa Foundation): an iPhone app at no charge that can notify you when an Aave position's
  health is low. Its last update, version 1.1.20, is dated 21 Oct 2024.
- **DeFi Saver Notify:** its help pages don't state a price.

Tokenping's alerts cost <AlertPrice />, paid on Arbitrum; looking at your positions needs no payment.

## Are browser extensions enough to monitor an Aave loan?

Browser extensions are enough only if you're usually at your computer: they show a toolbar badge or a desktop
notification while the browser is running, not a message on your phone. The two Aave extensions, Aave Monitor and Aave
DeFi Watcher, read data from Aave's contracts and need no wallet connection.

- **Aave Monitor** (Chrome Web Store; version 2.11.1, updated 24 Feb 2026) shows a starred address's health factor as a toolbar badge: green above 2, orange between 1 and 2, red
  below 1 by default, with thresholds you can change. It sends no push messages. For phones, its developer suggested iOS
  home-screen widgets built with the Scriptable app (July 2025); they refresh hourly by default but don't notify you.
- **Aave DeFi Watcher** (Chrome Web Store; version 1.0.0, updated 27 Jul 2026) adds desktop notifications to the badge.
  Its store listing says it alerts only when the risk level worsens, which keeps repeat notifications down. Its own site
  adds that it polls every 5 minutes by default and lists Aave V2, V3 and V4 on Ethereum, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base,
  Polygon and Avalanche, with Aave V4 limited to the Main Spoke on Ethereum.

A browser extension won't reach you while you're asleep or away from that computer. Message-based tools cover that gap:
Otomato, HF Guard, DeFi Monitor, DeFi Saver Notify, Ava and Tokenping.

## Which automation tools can repay Aave debt for you?

Three ready-made services checked here can repay Aave debt without you signing each transaction: DeFi Saver Automation,
Ava's Auto mode and Instadapp Protection Automation. The alternatives to DeFi Saver Automation for automatic Aave
repayment are Ava's Auto mode (Aave V3 on Ethereum and Base, no stated minimum) and Instadapp (Polygon and Avalanche,
status unclear). As of 19 Oct 2026:

- **DeFi Saver Automation** repays in two ways. Liquidation Protection repays part of the debt when the position's
  Safety Ratio, DeFi Saver's risk percentage, falls below a fixed minimum (105% on Aave V3, 110% on V4) and lifts it 5
  points. Automated Leverage Management repays at a ratio you choose, and it is the only DeFi Saver automation for
  positions with more than one collateral or debt asset. Both cover Aave V3 Core and V4 (not V3 Prime or EtherFi, and
  not Aave on Linea or Plasma) and need $30,000 of debt on Ethereum or $500 on Arbitrum, Optimism and Base. They work
  from a regular wallet (EOA) on Aave V3; on Aave V4 DeFi Saver's help says automations are for Smart Wallet positions
  and that EOA support is a goal. Each action costs a 0.05% automation fee plus a swap fee (0.25%, less between
  correlated assets), plus gas.
- **Ava's Auto mode** checks hourly, then tops up or repays from an Ava smart wallet you fund, under a permission that by
  default has no spending limit and lasts 365 days (the web app offers a narrower one). It covers Aave V3 on Ethereum
  and Base and charges nothing today.
- **Instadapp Protection Automation** repays toward a target health factor through an Instadapp smart account (DSA). Its
  guide lists Aave V3 on Polygon and Avalanche, a collateral minimum at activation of $1,000 (Polygon) or $5,000
  (Avalanche), and a fee of 0.3% to 0.4% per event, but also mentions a Mainnet minimum and Mainnet gas. The guide was
  last updated about 3 years ago, so current availability is unclear.

Automation has failure modes of its own. Ava's documentation notes that its Auto mode checks hourly, so a fast market can
move between checks. DeFi Saver's automation stays enabled but stops executing if the debt falls to $5,000 or less on
Ethereum, or $500 or less on Arbitrum, Base or Optimism. Instadapp's guide warns that losses are possible and tells users
to keep monitoring their positions.

## How can developers build their own Aave health factor alert?

A developer can build an Aave V3 health factor alert by calling `getUserAccountData(address)` on the market's Pool
contract on a schedule and sending a message when the returned health factor falls below a threshold. The value has 18
decimals, and the contract returns the maximum uint256 value when the address has no debt, which means there is nothing
to alert on. Tools that shorten the work, checked 19 Oct 2026:

- **Tenderly Alerts** can trigger on events, state changes or a view function's return value, and deliver to email,
  Slack, Telegram, Discord, webhooks, PagerDuty or Sentry. Alerts are on its paid plan only, through sales.
- **KeeperHub** documents an Aave V4 example (the Lido Spoke on Ethereum) that reads the health factor every 5 minutes
  and posts to Discord below 1.5. Its read-only actions work without credentials; its write actions need a connected
  wallet.
- **The OpenClaw skill "aave-liquidation-monitor"** (version 1.0.1, released 11 Feb 2026) checks Aave V3 positions every
  6 hours by default and sends alerts to Telegram, Discord or Slack. Its page names Ethereum as the main network and says
  it also works on Polygon, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, Avalanche and Gnosis. Agent skills run on your own machine, so it's
  worth reading the code before installing one.
- **Chainlink's CRE "automated liquidation protection" template** goes further: a generic lending template, not
  specific to Aave, in which an LLM chooses defensive actions and executes them. It depends on Confidential Workflows,
  which are in private beta.
- **Reactive Network's "Aave Unified Protection"** (blog post of 14 Oct 2025) describes contracts that add collateral or
  repay debt for subscribed Aave V3 positions. Its deployment steps and asset list are for the Sepolia testnet, and we
  found no mainnet deployment, so treat it as a developer example.

OpenZeppelin Defender, a hosted option for this kind of monitoring, closed sign-ups on 30 Jun 2025 and was scheduled to
retire on 1 Jul 2026. We found no later notice, and its app page loads without content, so we can't say whether it has
retired; OpenZeppelin's announcement points users to its open-source Monitor.

## What are the alternatives to HAL, Aave Alarm and Summer.fi?

No official HAL alternative exists: Aave removed the HAL notifications link from its interface in May 2024 because the
service had shut down, and Aave's help page now names only DeFi Simulator and DeFi Saver as health factor tools, both
marked "use at your own risk". Former users of HAL, Aave Alarm and Summer.fi now pick a third-party tool for the same
job:

- **Push alerts, as HAL and Aave Alarm sent:** Otomato (Telegram, phone app), HF Guard (Telegram), DeFi Monitor
  (Telegram, Discord), DeFi Saver Notify (email, Telegram, in-app) or Tokenping (Signal, Telegram, email).
- **Stop-loss, as Summer.fi offered:** DeFi Saver Automation has stop-loss and liquidation protection for Aave V3 Core
  and V4, subject to its debt minimums and, for both of those automations, one collateral and one debt asset.

## When is something other than Tokenping the better choice?

Another tool is the better choice if you don't want to pay for alerts, or you want a mobile app, a chat that answers
questions about your position, automatic repayment, a market or network Tokenping doesn't read, alerts on events other
than the health factor, or one message instead of repeats. In each case below, Tokenping can't do the job or another
tool does it with less friction.

- **You don't want to pay.** Tokenping's alerts cost <AlertPrice />. Otomato's core alerts come at no charge, HF Guard
  costs nothing during its beta, DeFi Monitor has a no-cost tier, and the Aave Monitor and Aave DeFi Watcher extensions
  cost nothing.
- **You want to watch an address you don't connect, or several wallets.** Tokenping watches only the signed-in wallet.
  Otomato, HF Guard, DeFi Monitor, Aave Monitor and Aave DeFi Watcher accept any pasted address.
- **You want a mobile app.** Tokenping has none; its alerts arrive in Signal, Telegram or your email app. Otomato has iOS
  and Android apps.
- **You want debt repaid automatically.** Tokenping only alerts. DeFi Saver Automation and Ava's Auto mode can repay
  for you.
- **You borrow on Aave V3 on Harmony or Fantom, or on a protocol other than Aave and Morpho.** Tokenping reads every
  Aave V3 market on a mainnet except the Harmony and Fantom markets that Aave froze in 2022, every Aave V4 Spoke and the
  Morpho markets the Morpho app lists, and nothing else. HF Guard, DeFi Monitor and DeFi Saver Notify also cover other
  lending protocols.
- **You want a chat that answers questions or shows your positions.** Tokenping's Telegram bot only sends alerts and
  understands /start, /stop and /help. Ava answers questions about your position in Telegram, and the HF Guard and DeFi
  Saver bots list your positions.
- **You want governance, supply-cap, rate-spike or depeg alerts.** Tokenping alerts on the health factor only. Otomato
  covers those events.
- **You want an alert at an asset price or a liquidation price rather than a health factor threshold.** Tokenping has no
  price alerts. Otomato also alerts on your distance to liquidation in dollars, and DeFi Saver's automation can act, not
  just alert, at a target price (repay on target price, stop-loss).
- **You want one message rather than repeats.** Tokenping repeats its alert every 5 minutes on each channel while the
  health factor stays below your threshold, sends no "recovered" message, and needs a separate alert for each market.
  Aave DeFi Watcher, by contrast, notifies only when risk worsens, and HF Guard alerts when a position changes risk
  level.
- **You need a long track record or published uptime.** Tokenping is in beta and publishes no uptime figures.

If none of these rules Tokenping out, [how to set up a health factor alert](/blog/aave-health-factor-alert-setup) walks
through the steps, and you can [check your Aave V3 health factor](/aave) before paying anything. The homepage has
an overview of [Aave health factor alerts on Signal, Telegram and email](/).

## How did we compile this comparison?

We compiled it from each tool's own pages, re-checked on 19 Oct 2026, without testing alert delivery. Tokenping wrote
this comparison and is one of the tools in it, so read it with that in mind. This is how we kept it as fair as we could:

1. Every claim about a live tool comes from that tool's own website, documentation, app-store listing or developer
   forum post, linked in its row. Three shutdown items rest on other sources: HAL's on Aave's own interface code change,
   Zapper's on news reports, and the cause of Summer.fi's wind-down on a news report. We didn't use third-party reviews.
2. Every row in the comparison and shutdown tables, and the check-interval table, comes from checks on 19 Oct 2026.
   Where a vendor's page doesn't state something, the table says "not stated" rather than guessing. Some vendor pages
   (Otomato, DeFi Monitor's pricing and changelog) only load in a browser, so we read them there.
3. We didn't create accounts with, or measure alert delivery on, any other tool. This compares stated features, not
   tested speed or reliability.
4. Tokenping's row comes from its [source code](https://github.com/romispectrum/tokenping), which is public so you can
   check what it does (re-read on 19 Oct 2026), an on-chain read of its payment contract on Arbitrum (block 516,226,427,
   19 Oct 2026) and a query of Aave's V4 API for the list of Spokes. The price shown on
   this page is read from that contract and refreshed at most hourly. We haven't tested the Telegram bot's behaviour
   beyond reading its code.
5. Tokenping's weaknesses are in its row and in the section on when to choose something else.
6. A search can miss a tool. We also looked for Aave health factor alerts at DeBank, Zerion, Push Protocol and Notifi
   and found none documented today, so they aren't listed. If we missed one, tell us.

We plan to re-check every row each quarter and to change the "Last checked" date only when we do. If a row is wrong or
out of date, tell us on [X](https://x.com/tokenping) or [Telegram](https://t.me/tokenping_xyz) and we'll correct it.
